Why Cannabis Tax Is Different

Section 280E changes what a cannabis business can deduct

Under Internal Revenue Code Section 280E, a business that traffics in a Schedule I or Schedule II controlled substance cannot deduct ordinary expenses such as rent, payroll, marketing or utilities, even when it is fully licensed under state law. What it can still do is subtract the cost of goods sold from its gross receipts. That one distinction is why inventory costing, not the usual hunt for write-offs, drives the tax bill for most cannabis businesses.

The practical result is a return where classification matters more than almost anything else: which costs belong in inventory, which are ordinary operating expenses, and whether your records can show the difference if the IRS ever asks.

Where the rules stand (reviewed October 2026)

The federal picture changed this year and is still moving. In April 2026 the Department of Justice moved FDA-approved cannabis products and cannabis under a qualifying state medical license to Schedule III, effective April 28, 2026, which takes that medical activity outside Section 280E. Adult-use cannabis stayed in Schedule I, so 280E continues to apply to adult-use sales. A DEA hearing on moving all cannabis to Schedule III ended in July without a final decision, and recent reports say the proceeding has been paused, so nobody should plan around a date.

If you hold both medical and adult-use activity, shared expenses may have to be allocated between the two. If you are adult-use only, nothing has changed yet. Whether and how relief applies to a given year depends on your license type and timing, so we review the facts before anyone files an amended return.

Ancillary businesses that never handle the plant, such as consultants, software providers or packaging suppliers, are often outside 280E, though where that line sits depends on the facts and is worth checking before you assume.

Common Questions

Frequently asked questions

Does Section 280E apply to my New Jersey cannabis business?+
It depends on your license. Section 280E applies to trafficking in Schedule I or II substances. Since April 28, 2026, cannabis under a qualifying state medical license and FDA-approved products sit in Schedule III, while adult-use cannabis remains in Schedule I. Many operators hold both, so shared costs may need to be allocated. Tell us your license types and we will confirm how your return should treat them.
What can a cannabis business still deduct under 280E?+
Cost of goods sold still reduces gross receipts, which is why inventory costing matters so much. Ordinary operating expenses are generally disallowed for activity covered by 280E. Deciding which costs qualify as inventory costs is the core of the work.
Do you help with cannabis licensing or state compliance?+
No. We handle tax preparation, bookkeeping and planning. Licensing and regulatory compliance are legal questions best handled by a cannabis attorney, and we are happy to coordinate with yours.
Can you work with a cash-heavy business?+
Yes. We tie daily deposits to sales reports, document how cash is handled and watch for payments that could require Form 8300 reporting.
How much does cannabis tax work cost?+
Cannabis returns vary with inventory complexity, license types and the condition of your books, so we quote a fixed price after reviewing your records. Our page on what tax preparation costs in Jersey City explains what moves a quote.
