The Self-Employed Tax Picture

No withholding means you are your own payroll department

If you are a freelancer, creative, consultant or gig worker, income reaches you gross. You report it on Schedule C, you owe self-employment tax on top of income tax to cover Social Security and Medicare, and you generally pay as you go through quarterly estimates to the IRS and to New Jersey. Underpay and penalties can follow. Overpay and you are lending the government money all year.

Good self-employed tax work is mostly timing and records. We build a payment schedule from your actual income rather than last year's guess, keep your deductions documented so they survive a question, and look at the structure of the business before the year gets away from you.

The mechanics are laid out in our guide to quarterly estimated taxes for NJ freelancers. This page is for getting someone to do it with you.

Choosing a Structure

LLC, S-corp and what a defensible salary looks like

A single-member LLC is taxed like a sole proprietorship by default. An S-corp election can reduce self-employment tax on profit above a reasonable salary, but it adds payroll, a separate return and, in New Jersey, state-level elections to get right. Whether it pays depends on profit, not on how established you feel.

Start with our comparison of LLC vs. S-corp in New Jersey, then read how to think about S-corp reasonable compensation in New Jersey. If your profit is climbing and you are not sure whether to elect, that is a planning conversation we have through tax planning and consulting.

Common Questions

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to make quarterly estimated payments as a freelancer?+
If you expect to owe tax after withholding, generally yes, to both the IRS and New Jersey. The usual due dates are mid-April, mid-June, mid-September and mid-January. We set a schedule based on your actual income.
What can a freelancer deduct?+
Ordinary and necessary business costs such as software, equipment, a qualifying home office, mileage and professional fees, plus items like self-employed health insurance and retirement contributions. The key is documentation, which is where we help.
When does an S-corp make sense for a freelancer?+
Usually when profit is high enough that the self-employment tax saved on income above a reasonable salary outweighs the added payroll and filing costs. We run the numbers on your actual profit.
I get paid through platforms. Do I still report that income?+
Yes. Income reported on a 1099-K, 1099-NEC or not reported at all is taxable, and mismatches are a common source of notices. We reconcile your platform statements to your return.
How much does an accountant for freelancers cost?+
We quote a fixed price from your documents, not by the hour. See what tax preparation costs in Jersey City for what moves the number.
