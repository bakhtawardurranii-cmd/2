Haven't Filed Taxes in Years? How to Catch Up in New Jersey

Quick Answer

File your missing returns, even if you cannot pay. The IRS generally enforces about six years of unfiled returns, penalties keep growing until you file, and a refund can be lost after three years. The IRS says to file all past-due returns whether or not you can pay in full.

Will the IRS come after you?

There is no deadline that makes an unfiled return go away. The IRS clock to assess tax generally does not start until you file. The IRS can also prepare a substitute return for you, and it uses only the income it has on record, so it usually leaves out deductions and credits.

What does it cost to wait?

The failure-to-file penalty is 5% of unpaid tax for each month or part of a month, up to 25%. The failure-to-pay penalty is 0.5% per month, also up to 25%. When both apply in the same month, the combined charge is 5% a month, and interest is added on top.

Example: you owe $4,000 and file five months late. The combined penalties reach the 25% cap, about $1,000, before interest. The late-payment penalty then keeps running at about $20 a month. This is an illustration, not a quote for your situation.

How many years do you need to file?

For individuals, IRS policy generally limits enforcement to the last six years of unfiled returns. The IRS can ask for more if you owe significant tax in earlier years, or for business returns. Confirm which years the IRS expects before you file anything.

Step by step: how to file back taxes

  1. List every year you did not file, including state returns.
  2. Pull your income records. Request wage and income transcripts with Form 4506-T, line 8, or use your IRS online account.
  3. Gather what the transcripts miss: self-employment income, expenses, rental records, and mortgage statements.
  4. Prepare each year on that year's forms. Do not reuse one year's numbers for another.
  5. File the returns. Pay what you can with each one and ask about payment options for the rest.
  6. Set reminders or quarterly payments so you stay current.

Can you still get a refund?

Possibly, but not for old years. A refund generally must be claimed within three years of the return due date. After that it is lost. This is the main reason to start with your most recent years.

What if the IRS already filed a return for you?

You can file your own return to replace it. If you received a notice, follow the response date on it. See our guide to what to do with an IRS or NJ tax notice. If you only missed one deadline, read filing taxes late in New Jersey instead.

Common Questions

Frequently asked questions

Will I go to jail for not filing taxes in years?+
Failing to file is usually handled with penalties and interest. Willful evasion is a different matter and can be criminal. Filing voluntarily and accurately is the safest path.
Do I have to file if I cannot pay?+
Yes. File anyway. The failure-to-file penalty is much larger than the failure-to-pay penalty, and the IRS offers payment options.
Do I need to file New Jersey returns too?+
If you had New Jersey income or were a resident and met the filing threshold, yes. We prepare federal and NJ returns together.
