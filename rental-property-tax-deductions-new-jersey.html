Rental Property Tax Deductions for New Jersey Landlords

Know what you can deduct now, what must be depreciated, and what records to keep.

Rental Property Tax Deductions for New Jersey Landlords

Quick Answer

Landlords report rental income and expenses on Schedule E. Repairs are generally deducted in the year paid. Improvements are depreciated, and residential rental buildings use a 27.5-year recovery period.

What rental expenses can you deduct?

IRS Publication 527 covers residential rental property. Common deductible costs include:

  • Mortgage interest and property taxes
  • Landlord insurance
  • Utilities you pay
  • Repairs and maintenance
  • Property management and advertising fees
  • Accounting and tax preparation fees for the rental

Repairs vs. improvements

This is where landlords most often go wrong. A repair keeps the property in working order. An improvement makes it better, restores it, or adapts it to a new use.

  • Repair, deducted now: repainting, fixing a leak, patching a wall, replacing one broken window with a similar one
  • Improvement, depreciated: a new roof, replacing all windows with upgraded ones, a kitchen remodel
  • If repairs are part of a larger remodel, the whole job may count as an improvement

How depreciation works

Depreciation lets you deduct the cost of the building over time. Land is not depreciable, and you can start once the unit is ready and available for rent.

Example: a rental building with a $270,000 cost basis (excluding land) divided by 27.5 years gives about $9,818 of depreciation a year. This is an illustration only. Your basis depends on your purchase price, closing costs and improvements.

Renting part of your home

Own a two-family and live in one unit? Only the rental share of shared costs, such as taxes, insurance and repairs, goes on Schedule E. The rest stays on your personal return. See NJ homeowner tax deductions and the SALT cap for NJ homeowners for the personal side.

Short-term rentals

Vacation and short-term rentals have different rules. Read reporting vacation rental income first.

Records to keep

  • Rent received, by month and by unit
  • Receipts and invoices for every expense
  • Closing statement from the purchase
  • Dates and costs of every improvement
  • Mortgage interest and property tax statements

New Jersey also taxes rental income on the state return, so the numbers on both returns need to agree. We prepare rental property tax returns for Jersey City landlords and can set up depreciation for you.

Common Questions

Frequently asked questions

Can I deduct the cost of a new roof right away?+
Generally no. A new roof is an improvement, which is depreciated over time rather than deducted in one year.
When does depreciation start?+
When the property is ready and available for rent, even if you do not yet have a tenant.
Do I report rental income in New Jersey too?+
Yes. Net rental income is part of your New Jersey return. We prepare both.
