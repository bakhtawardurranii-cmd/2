Local Context

Rental income, seasonal cash flow and estimated payments

Toms River and the barrier-island towns nearby are summer-rental country. Rental income is generally reported on Schedule E, and expenses are divided between rental and personal use based on how many days each applies. If you rent a home for 14 days or fewer in a year, the income is generally not taxable, but that rule has limits, so check it before you rely on it.

Seasonal business owners and self-employed filers face a different problem: income that lands in a few months, while tax is due quarterly. We build an estimated-payment schedule around your real season. See our guides on reporting vacation rental income and quarterly estimated taxes. New Jersey also applies sales tax and occupancy fees to many short-term rentals, separate from income tax. Whether they apply depends on how you rent, so confirm before the season starts.

Common Questions

Frequently asked questions

I rent my shore house for part of the summer. How is the income reported?+
Rental income is generally reported on Schedule E, with expenses allocated between rental and personal days. There is a limited exception for renting 14 days or fewer a year, so we check which rules apply to you.
I use the house myself too. How are the expenses handled?+
Generally, expenses are divided between rental and personal use by days, and heavy personal use can limit the deductions you can claim. We work out the split from your calendar.
My business is busy only in summer. How should I pay estimated tax?+
Estimated payments don't have to be even if your income isn't. We can build a schedule around your actual season so you avoid an underpayment penalty without overpaying early.
Do I need to come to Jersey City?+
No. Toms River clients work with us entirely online, by phone, video and secure upload. In-person meetings are available by appointment at our Jersey City office.
