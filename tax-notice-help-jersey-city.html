Start Here

Do not ignore it, and do not pay it blindly

Most notices come from automated matching, comparing what you reported with what employers, banks or clients reported. Sometimes the notice is right. Sometimes the mismatch is a missing form, a credit that was not claimed or a number entered in the wrong place. Either way, the notice states a response date, often 30 days, and missing it can limit your options to dispute a proposed change.

The IRS generally makes first contact by mail. Be cautious about phone calls, texts or emails that demand immediate payment, and never pay by gift card or wire based on an unsolicited demand. If you are not sure a letter is real, send us a photo and we will help you check.

For a plain-language walk-through of the first steps, read our guide on what to do if you get an IRS or NJ Division of Taxation notice. This page is for when you would rather have someone look at your letter with you.

Being Clear About Scope

What we do, and when we will point you elsewhere

We help you read the notice, collect documents, prepare a written response and file any return or correction it calls for. We do not represent you before the IRS or the state in audits, appeals or collection cases. If your situation needs formal representation, such as an enrolled agent, CPA or tax attorney, we will tell you plainly and hand over everything we have prepared.

Common Questions

Frequently asked questions

I got a notice from the IRS. What should I do first?+
Read the whole letter, note the response date and do not ignore it. Then send us a photo and we will explain what it is and what it asks for.
Does a notice mean I am being audited?+
Not usually. Most notices are automated corrections or requests for documentation. A few are exams, and we will tell you if yours looks like one that needs a credentialed representative.
Will you speak to the IRS for me?+
We do not represent clients in audits, appeals or collections. We help you understand the notice and prepare your response, and refer you to an enrolled agent, CPA or tax attorney if representation is needed.
What if I have not filed for a few years?+
File as soon as you can. Penalties and interest keep growing the longer a return sits unfiled, and a prepared return gives you a real number to plan around.
