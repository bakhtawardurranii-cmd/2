Start Here

Do not ignore it, and do not pay it blindly

Most notices come from automated matching, comparing what you reported with what employers, banks or clients reported. Sometimes the notice is right. Sometimes the mismatch is a missing form, a credit that was not claimed or a number entered in the wrong place. Either way, the notice states a response date, often 30 days, and missing it can limit your options to dispute a proposed change.

The IRS generally makes first contact by mail. Be cautious about phone calls, texts or emails that demand immediate payment, and never pay by gift card or wire based on an unsolicited demand. If you are not sure a letter is real, send us a photo and we will help you check.

For a plain-language walk-through of the first steps, read our guide on what to do if you get an IRS or NJ Division of Taxation notice. This page is for when you would rather have someone look at your letter with you.

Representation

When you want us to deal with the IRS for you

Our firm holds the credentials to represent clients before the IRS. With a signed power of attorney (Form 2848), we can speak with the IRS on your behalf, receive copies of your notices, request time to respond and handle correspondence, so you are not the one on hold or reading the fine print. That covers exam and audit letters, balance due and collection notices, payment plan requests and the paperwork to get back into compliance.

Representation is not a promise of a particular result. The IRS decides, and some balances are simply owed. What we can promise is that the facts are presented accurately, deadlines are met and you hear the realistic options, including whether a payment plan, penalty relief or no action at all fits your situation, before you commit to anything.

Many notices never reach that stage. Correcting the underlying return with tax preparation in Jersey City or sorting out your books with bookkeepers in Jersey City often resolves it and helps prevent the same letter next year. For New Jersey Division of Taxation letters, we prepare the response and any filing it calls for and send it with your authorization.

Common Questions

Frequently asked questions

I got a notice from the IRS. What should I do first?+
Read the whole letter, note the response date and do not ignore it. Then send us a photo and we will explain what it is and what it asks for.
Does a notice mean I am being audited?+
Not usually. Most notices are automated corrections or requests for documentation. If yours is an actual exam, we can represent you through it.
Can you speak to the IRS for me?+
Yes. Our firm is credentialed to represent clients before the IRS. With a signed Form 2848 power of attorney we can talk to the IRS, receive your notices and handle audits, balance due and collection matters for you.
What if I have not filed for a few years?+
File as soon as you can. Penalties and interest keep growing the longer a return sits unfiled, and a prepared return gives you a real number to plan around.
How much does notice help or IRS representation cost?+
A straightforward notice response is usually a modest fixed fee, while audits and collection cases are scoped individually. We quote after seeing the notice and your documents. See what tax preparation costs in Jersey City for typical ranges.
